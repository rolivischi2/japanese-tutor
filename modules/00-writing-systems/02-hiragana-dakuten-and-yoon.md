# Hiragana — Dakuten, Handakuten, and Yōon

Once the 46 base hiragana are solid, the rest of the script is **modifications of symbols you already know**. No genuinely new shapes here — only two small diacritics and a small-kana shrinking trick. Three days.

- **Day 6** — dakuten and handakuten (the voiced rows).
- **Day 7** — yōon (the small-ゃゅょ combinations).
- **Day 8** — review + first sight-reading speed test.

---

## Dakuten — the voicing mark ゛

A **dakuten** is two short strokes added to the top-right of a kana. It **voices** the consonant — the vocal cords switch on. The vowel and the symbol's body are unchanged, so you are not learning new shapes, just a new flag on familiar ones.

| Base row | + dakuten | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| か き く け こ | **が ぎ ぐ げ ご** | k → g [g] |
| さ し す せ そ | **ざ じ ず ぜ ぞ** | s → z [z] (じ/ず special, below) |
| た ち つ て と | **だ ぢ づ で ど** | t → d [d] (ぢ/づ special, below) |
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | **ば び ぶ べ ぼ** | h → b [b] |

The voicing logic is the same voiced/voiceless consonant pairing found in most European languages — *k/g*, *s/z*, *t/d*, *p/b* — a familiar contrast, just spelled with a diacritic.

### Special cases in the voiced rows

- **じ and ぢ** both sound [(d)ʑi] — essentially identical in modern standard Japanese. **づ and ず** both sound [(d)zɯ] — also identical. This is the *yotsugana* merger. In practice じ and ず are the everyday spellings; ぢ and づ appear only in a few words (e.g. compounds where a づ-sound arises from つ). For reading, treat the pairs as homophones; for the rare writing case, prefer じ/ず unless a word specifically uses ぢ/づ.
- **ざ ず ぜ ぞ** — the z is a plain [z] as in English *zero* or German *See*. じ is the soft [(d)ʑ], like the *j* of an English "jeep" softened.

Example words:
- かぎ — "key"
- みず — "water"
- でんわ — "telephone"
- だれ — "who"

## Handakuten — the half-voicing mark ゜

A **handakuten** is a small circle at the top-right. It applies **only to the は-row**, turning h → p.

| Base row | + handakuten | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | **ぱ ぴ ぷ ぺ ぽ** | h → p [p] |

The p is a plain unaspirated [p] — like *p* in Spanish or Italian (not the aspirated English *p*). Visually: **゛dakuten = two strokes** (b-sound), **゜handakuten = one circle** (p-sound). Do not confuse them; ば and ぱ are different words apart.

Example words:
- ぱん — "bread"
- えんぴつ — "pencil"
- さんぽ — "a walk / a stroll"

The full voiced + half-voiced set is 25 symbols — but again, **zero new shapes**. If the base 46 are solid, this is one short day.

## Yōon — the small-ゃゅょ combinations

**Yōon** are *contracted / palatalised* syllables: a kana from the **i-column** (き し ち に ひ み り and their voiced forms) followed by a **small** や, ゆ, or よ. The combination is read as a **single mora** — one beat, not two.

The rule is mechanical:
- The i-column kana keeps its consonant but **drops its [i]**.
- The small ゃ/ゅ/ょ supplies the vowel.
- き [ki] + small ゃ [ja] → **きゃ** [kja] — one mora.

The size matters: large や ゆ よ are full separate morae; small ゃ ゅ ょ fuse with the preceding kana. Compare:
- **きや** — two morae [ki-ja]
- **きゃ** — one mora [kja]

This contrast is real and lexical; read the size carefully.

### The yōon table

| Base (i-column) | + ゃ | + ゅ | + ょ |
|---|---|---|---|
| き | きゃ [kja] | きゅ [kjɯ] | きょ [kjo] |
| し | しゃ [ɕa] | しゅ [ɕɯ] | しょ [ɕo] |
| ち | ちゃ [tɕa] | ちゅ [tɕɯ] | ちょ [tɕo] |
| に | にゃ [ɲa] | にゅ [ɲɯ] | にょ [ɲo] |
| ひ | ひゃ [ça] | ひゅ [çɯ] | ひょ [ço] |
| み | みゃ [mja] | みゅ [mjɯ] | みょ [mjo] |
| り | りゃ [ɾja] | りゅ [ɾjɯ] | りょ [ɾjo] |
| ぎ | ぎゃ [gja] | ぎゅ [gjɯ] | ぎょ [gjo] |
| じ | じゃ [(d)ʑa] | じゅ [(d)ʑɯ] | じょ [(d)ʑo] |
| び | びゃ [bja] | びゅ [bjɯ] | びょ [bjo] |
| ぴ | ぴゃ [pja] | ぴゅ [pjɯ] | ぴょ [pjo] |

Note that しゃ/しゅ/しょ and ちゃ/ちゅ/ちょ have **no extra [j] glide** — し and ち are already palatal, so these are simply [ɕa ɕɯ ɕo] and [tɕa tɕɯ tɕo]. (ぢ + small kana exists but is vanishingly rare; じゃ/じゅ/じょ are the standard spellings for that sound.)

Example words:
- おちゃ — "tea"
- かいしゃ — "company / workplace"
- りょこう — "trip / travel"
- でんしゃ — "train"
- きょう — "today"

## Mora counting — get this right now

This skill matters for the speed test, for pronunciation timing, and for everything later. Each of the following is **one mora / one beat**:

- a plain kana — か
- a yōon — きゃ
- the moraic nasal — ん
- a long-vowel second kana — the う in きょう
- (introduced visually in `03`/`04`) the small っ sokuon, and the ー mark

So きょう = **two** morae: きょ + う. でんしゃ = **three** morae: で + ん + しゃ. Counting morae, not symbols, is how you read at an even Japanese rhythm. The deep treatment is `pronunciation/01-mora-and-timing.md`.

## End of hiragana

After Day 7 you have the **entire hiragana system**: 46 base + 25 dakuten/handakuten + 33 yōon. Day 8 is review and your **first 40 mora/min speed test** (see `00-overview.md`) — drill on realkana.com with every hiragana option ticked, including dakuten and yōon. Clear 40 mora/min before starting katakana in `03`. Vocabulary is collected in `module-vocab.json`.
