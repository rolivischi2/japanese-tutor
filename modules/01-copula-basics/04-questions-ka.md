# Questions — か

## The whole rule: add か at the end

To turn a statement into a yes/no question, add **か** ([ka](module-vocab.json#ka))
to the very end of the sentence:

> あなた は がくせい **です**。
> You are a student.

> あなた は がくせい **です か**。
> Are you a student?

That is the entire rule. か is a spoken question mark.

## Word order does NOT change

This is the big contrast with German and English. Those languages **invert** to
ask a question:

- German: "Du bist Student." → "**Bist** du Student?"
- English: "You are a student." → "**Are** you a student?"

Japanese moves **nothing**. The statement keeps its exact order; か is simply
appended. There is no inversion, no auxiliary verb hopping to the front. か at
the end does the entire job.

## Punctuation

か already marks the sentence as a question, so standard Japanese writing ends a
か-question with a normal full stop 。 — the か itself is the "question mark."
You will often see a "?" added for clarity in textbooks and worksheets (your
Preply PDF does this); both are fine. This module uses 。.

## Answering: はい / いいえ + a full sentence

Answer with はい ([hai](module-vocab.json#hai), "yes") or いいえ
([iie](module-vocab.json#iie), "no"), then — in a lesson — give a **full
sentence**, not just the bare word:

> あなた は がくせい です か。
> Are you a student?
> — はい、がくせい です。
> — Yes, I am (a student).
> — いいえ、がくせい じゃありません。
> — No, I am not (a student).

In real fast conversation はい on its own is fine. But while drilling, say the
whole sentence — it builds the structure into your mouth.

## Negative questions, briefly

You can also question a negative — just add か to a ではありません / じゃありません
sentence:

> マリオ は にほんじん じゃありません か。
> Mario is not Japanese, is he? / Isn't Mario Japanese?

Answering negative questions in Japanese can flip from the English habit
(Japanese はい can mean "yes, what you said is correct"). Do not over-drill this
in Module 01 — just be aware of it. Stick to plain affirmative questions for now.

## Examples

> あなた は エンジニア です か。
> Are you an engineer?
> — はい、エンジニア です。 — Yes, I am.

> あなた は にほんじん です か。
> Are you Japanese?
> — いいえ、にほんじん じゃありません。 — No, I am not.

> これ は コーヒー です か。
> Is this coffee?
> — はい、コーヒー です。 — Yes, it is.

> それ は あなたの スマホ です か。
> Is that (by you) your smartphone?
> — はい、わたしの スマホ です。 — Yes, it is mine.

> あの ひと は せんせい です か。
> Is that person a teacher?
> — いいえ、せんせい じゃありません。がくせい です。
> — No, not a teacher. A student.

## Culture note — あなた

Grammatically あなた ([anata](module-vocab.json#anata)) means "you," and the
drills use it for practice. But native speakers usually **avoid あなた** and use
the person's **name + さん** instead:

> マリオさん は がくせい です か。
> Is Mario a student?

In real conversation, prefer the name. See file 05 and `dialogues.md`.

## Pronunciation: question intonation

A yes/no question ends on a **rising pitch** — the melody lifts on か, much like
a German or English question. です か → か goes up.

Keep this separate in your head from word **pitch accent**: pitch accent is the
fixed high/low shape inside a single word; question **intonation** is the rising
melody over the whole sentence. Both exist at once. See
`pronunciation/05-pitch-accent-intro.md`.

## Common mistakes

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| か です あなた がくせい | あなた は がくせい です か | No reordering — か just goes last. |
| inverting like German | keep statement order + か | Japanese does not invert for questions. |
| いきます か です | いきます か | か goes after the verb; no です on a verb (file 01). |
| answering with bare はい in a drill | はい、〜です。 | Drill the full sentence. |

## Quick check

Answer each question with a full sentence (answer truthfully).

1. あなた は がくせい です か。
2. これ は パン です か。 *(it is coffee)*
3. あなた は にほんじん です か。

> 1. はい、がくせい です。 / いいえ、がくせい じゃありません。
> 2. いいえ、パン じゃありません。コーヒー です。
> 3. いいえ、にほんじん じゃありません。
