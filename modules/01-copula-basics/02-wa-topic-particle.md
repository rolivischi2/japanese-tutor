# は — The Topic Particle

## は is written "ha" but read "wa"

The particle は ([wa](module-vocab.json#wa-particle)) is written with the
hiragana は, but when it works as a particle it is **always pronounced "wa."**

This is a fixed spelling rule, not an exception you have to memorise
word-by-word. It is the same reason こんに**ち**は ("hello") ends in a "wa"
sound — that final は is the topic particle frozen into the greeting.

So: see は as a particle → say "wa." See は inside a normal word (はい, ほん is
not は) → say "ha." In this module は is always the particle, so it is always
"wa."

## What は actually does: it marks the *topic*

It is tempting to call は "the subject marker." It is not. **は marks the
topic.** The cleanest English translation is:

> **は = "as for ___"**

So:

> わたし は がくせい です。
> *As for me, (it is) student.*  →  "I am a student."

> にほん は きれい です。
> *As for Japan, (it is) beautiful.*  →  "Japan is beautiful."

は picks something out and announces "here is what I am going to talk about."
Everything after it is the comment on that topic.

## Topic is not subject — the Cure Dolly framing

Here is the framing worth installing early (it pays off in Module 02 and
beyond). Every Japanese sentence has, underneath, a **が-marked subject** — the
actual doer or the thing being described. は does something different: it lifts
a noun up and presents it as the **topic**.

Often the topic *and* the subject are the same thing, so it looks like は just
marks the subject:

> わたし は がくせい です。
> Topic = わたし. Underlying subject = わたし. They coincide here.

But the underlying が-subject **can be elided** (left unsaid) once the topic
makes it obvious. And the topic does **not have to be** the subject — in later
modules you will meet sentences where は presents something that is not the
doer at all. For now, the takeaway is just this:

- は **presents a topic** ("as for X…").
- The real subject is a separate, often-invisible が role.
- In Module 01's simple `N は N です` sentences the two happen to line up — but
  treat は as "topic," not "subject," so the later modules make sense.

You do not need が yet. が is introduced properly in Module 02. Just do not
*mislabel* は as the subject marker in the meantime.

## You may not drop は

In casual fast speech particles sometimes vanish (Module 07). Not now. In
polite Module 01 sentences, keep は in:

> ✗ わたし がくせい です。
> ✓ わたし は がくせい です。

## は vs が — a one-line preview

You will eventually ask "when は, when が?" The short, incomplete answer:
は introduces *known / already-on-the-table* information as a topic; が
introduces *new* information or answers "who/what?" The full contrast is
Module 02. For Module 01, every sentence uses は.

## Examples

> わたし は スイスじん です。
> I am Swiss.

> ミカさん は せんせい です。
> Mika is a teacher.

> これ は わたしの ほん です。
> This is my book.

> あの ひと は ともだち です。
> That person is a friend.

> きょう は やすみ です。
> Today is a day off.  *(は can present a time, not just a person.)*

## Pronunciation

は as a particle is one light mora, "wa" [wa]. Do not stress it — in Hungarian
the instinct is to thump the first syllable; here every mora stays equally
loud, and a grammatical particle especially should stay light and quick. See
`pronunciation/07-hungarian-transfer-notes.md`.

## Quick check

Read aloud and translate. Watch the pronunciation of は.

1. わたし は エンジニア です。
2. にほん は きれい です。
3. これ は コーヒー です。

> 1. "watashi WA enjinia desu." — I am an engineer.
> 2. "nihon WA kirei desu." — Japan is beautiful.
> 3. "kore WA koohii desu." — This is coffee.
