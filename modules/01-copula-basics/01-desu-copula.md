# です — The Polite Copula

## The core sentence

The whole module is built on one skeleton:

> **N₁ は N₂ です。**
> N₁ is N₂.

> わたし は がくせい です。
> I am a student.

`わたし` ([watashi](module-vocab.json#watashi)) is the topic, `がくせい`
([gakusei](module-vocab.json#gakusei)) is the noun, `です` ([desu](module-vocab.json#desu))
is the copula. The next file covers は; this file is about です itself.

## です links two nouns — it does not mean "to exist"

English "to be" does two different jobs:

- **Identity:** "I *am* a teacher." (I belong to the category *teacher*.)
- **Existence:** "There *is* a teacher." (A teacher exists somewhere.)

Japanese splits these into two separate words. **です only does identity.**
Think of です as an equals sign:

> わたし = がくせい

For existence ("there is / there are") Japanese uses あります and います — and
that is Module 02. So a useful rule to lock in now: **です never means
"exists."** It only connects N₁ and N₂ and says they are the same thing, or
that N₁ falls inside the category N₂.

This matters because the German *sein* and English *be* will tempt you to reach
for です whenever you want "is." Resist it. です is the identity link, nothing
more.

## です never inflects

One form covers every person, number, and gender. There is no am/is/are split
and no plural -s:

> わたし は がくせい です。
> I am a student.

> ミカ は がくせい です。
> Mika is a student.

> マリオ と ミカ は がくせい です。
> Mario and Mika are students.

Compared to German (*ich bin, du bist, er ist, wir sind*), this is a genuine
simplification — there is simply nothing to conjugate here.

## Word order: the copula goes last

Japanese sentences are copula-final. です sits at the very end, after the
noun. This never changes:

- Statement: `わたし は エンジニア です。`
- Negative: `わたし は エンジニア ではありません。` (file 03)
- Question: `わたし は エンジニア ですか。` (file 04)

In German and English the verb moves for questions ("*Bist* du…?", "*Are*
you…?"). Japanese moves nothing. The ending changes; the order does not. Hold
onto this — it is one of the most reliable structural facts in the language.

## ✗ Never attach です to a verb

> ✗ いきますです
> ✓ いきます

です attaches to **nouns** (and, later, to な-adjectives). It does **not**
attach to verbs. A verb like いきます already carries its own politeness, so
adding です is doubly wrong. Verbs arrive in Module 04 — until then, if a です
ever lands on something that is not a noun, it is a mistake.

## More examples

> わたし は ローラント です。
> I am Roland.

> わたし は エンジニア です。
> I am an engineer.

> わたし は スイスじん です。
> I am Swiss.

> これ は ほん です。
> This is a book.

> ミカさん は ともだち です。
> Mika is a friend.

> わたし は さんじゅうなな さい です。
> I am thirty-seven years old.

**Note on age — さい.** Most ages are *number + さい* ([sai](module-vocab.json#sai)).
Three sound changes: 1 → いっさい, 8 → はっさい, 10 → じゅっさい. And **20 years
old is irregular: はたち** (you will also hear にじゅっさい, but はたち is the
standard word).

## Pronunciation: です sounds like "dess"

The final す is usually **devoiced** — the [ɯ] almost disappears and you are
left with a soft "s." So です is closer to [des] than [desɯ]. And the う itself,
when it does sound, is **not** the rounded Hungarian/German /u/: Japanese /u/ is
compressed and near-unrounded [ɯ̜] — do not push your lips forward. This is
your single most salient vowel trap (see `pronunciation/02-the-five-vowels.md`).

Mora timing: です = で・す, two equal beats. Tap one beat per kana.

## A note on plain だ (preview only)

です is the **polite** copula. There is also a **plain** copula, **だ**, used
with friends and family (わたし は がくせい だ). You will hear it constantly in
listening practice. **Do not produce it yet** — Module 01 through 05 stay
polite so you can use everything directly in Preply lessons. Plain だ gets its
full treatment in Module 06. For now: recognise it, don't use it.
