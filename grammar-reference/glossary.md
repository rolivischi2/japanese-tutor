## mora
TODO: Define: the unit of duration in Japanese. One mora = one beat in the clap-per-beat drill. Special morae: ん (moraic nasal), っ (geminate marker), long vowel extension (ー). Contrast with syllable.

## particle
TODO: Define: a short grammatical word that follows a noun, verb, or adjective and marks its grammatical role or adds nuance. Japanese particles are post-positional (they come after the word they modify). Examples: は、が、を、に、で.

## copula
TODO: Define: a verb that links a subject to a predicate (noun or adjective). In Japanese: polite form = です; plain form = だ. Distinct from English "to be" — it does not conjugate for person or number.

## godan (五段)
TODO: Define: Group 1 / u-verbs. Conjugate through all five vowel rows of the kana chart. Identified by any final vowel-consonant combination where the final mora is not い/え-row + る. Also called "u-verbs."

## ichidan (一段)
TODO: Define: Group 2 / ru-verbs. Conjugate through a single vowel row (only the い or え row). End in い-row or え-row + る. Also called "ru-verbs."

## te-form
TODO: Define: a conjunctive verb form ending in て or で. Used for sequencing, requests, progressive aspect, permission/prohibition, and 10+ other grammatical constructions. The most productive form in Japanese grammar.

## sokuon (促音)
TODO: Define: the geminate consonant marker っ. Represents a "held" mora (one full beat of closure) before the following consonant is released. The doubled consonant begins on the next mora.

## dakuten (濁点)
TODO: Define: the two dots (゛) added to certain kana to indicate voicing. Example: か → が (k→g); さ → ざ (s→z); た → だ (t→d); は → ば (h→b).

## handakuten (半濁点)
TODO: Define: the small circle (゜) added to は-row kana to indicate the p-sound. Example: は → ぱ、ひ → ぴ.

## yōon (拗音)
TODO: Define: compound kana formed by combining a consonant kana with small や、ゆ、よ. Example: き + ゃ = きゃ (kya). Counts as one mora, not two.

## plain form
TODO: Define: the dictionary/base register of Japanese — used in casual speech, subordinate clauses, journaling, internal monologue. Verb dictionary form, adjective citation form, だ copula.

## polite form
TODO: Define: the ます/です register — appropriate for strangers, service interactions, professional contexts, and Preply lessons. Required in M01–05 output.

## honorific
TODO: Define: forms of speech that elevate the perceived status of the subject or the addressee. In Japanese: 尊敬語 (sonkeigo) elevates others; 謙譲語 (kenjōgo) lowers the speaker. Covered in M11.

## humble (謙譲語)
TODO: Define: forms that lower the speaker's actions as a sign of respect toward the listener or subject. Example: いただく (humble receive), まいる (humble go/come).

## transitive (他動詞, tadōshi)
TODO: Define: a verb that takes a direct object (marked with を). Example: たべる (eat [something]).

## intransitive (自動詞, jidōshi)
TODO: Define: a verb that does not take a direct object. Often comes in transitive/intransitive pairs with different forms. Example: あく (open [intransitive]) vs あける (open [transitive]).

## aspect
TODO: Define: grammatical marking of whether an action is completed, ongoing, or has a resultant state — distinct from tense (time). In Japanese: 〜ている can mark progressive aspect or resultant state.

## copular sentence vs verbal sentence

## topic vs subject
TODO: Define: は marks the topic (what the sentence is about — can be the subject, object, time, or anything elevated for discourse focus). が marks the grammatical subject (the agent or experiencer of the verb). The topic is a discourse-level concept; the subject is a clause-level syntactic one.
