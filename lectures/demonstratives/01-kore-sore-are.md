# これ / それ / あれ / どれ

Japanese demonstratives split on a **four-way distance** system. Three
of the words name a thing; the fourth asks "which one?".

| Word | Distance | Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **これ** | near the speaker | "this (here, by me)" |
| **それ** | near the listener | "that (by you)" |
| **あれ** | far from both | "that (over there)" |
| **どれ** | unknown | "which (of these)?" |

English collapses これ and それ both into "this/that" depending on
which speaker is doing the pointing — Japanese is more precise about
**whose side** the thing is on.

## Pattern

```
これ / それ / あれ は X です 。
```

These words are **nouns** — they stand alone and take は (or any other
particle). They don't attach to another noun directly.

## Examples

> これ は ほん です。
> *This is a book.* (the book is by me)

> それ は ペン です。
> *That is a pen.* (the pen is by you)

> あれ は がっこう です。
> *That over there is a school.* (it's far from both of us)

> どれ が やまださん の かばん ですか。
> *Which one is Yamada-san's bag?*

> これ は わたし の ほん です。
> *This is my book.*

> それ は にほんご の じしょ ですか。
> *Is that a Japanese dictionary?*

## Quick preview — この / その / あの / どの

There's a parallel series that **modifies** a following noun instead of
standing alone:

> この ほん は おもしろい です。
> *This book is interesting.*

> その ペン を ください。
> *Please give me that pen.*

The rule: **これ stands alone**, **この needs a noun right after it**.
You'll see a full lecture on the こ/そ/あ/ど chart later.

<details><summary>Why is あの ひと, not あれ ひと, the way to say "that person"?</summary>あれ stands alone (must be followed by a particle). To attach directly to a noun you switch to the modifying form **あの**: あの ひと.</details>
