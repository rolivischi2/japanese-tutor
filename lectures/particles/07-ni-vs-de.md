# に vs で — the "at" trap

Both に and で translate as English **"at"** or **"in"** for places. The
mistake every beginner makes is picking the one that matches the
preposition in English. Don't do that — pick based on the **verb**.

## The rule

- **Action verb** (do something at a place) → **で**
- **Existence verb** (be / exist at a place) → **に**
- **Motion-toward verb** (go / come / move to a place) → **に**

The verb decides; the noun is innocent.

## Same place, three particles

The classic demonstration: take one place (こうえん, "the park") and
swap the verb.

> こうえん **に** いきます。
> *I go **to** the park.* (motion toward → に)

> こうえん **で** あそびます。
> *I play **in** the park.* (action happens there → で)

> こうえん **に** います。
> *I'm **at** the park.* (just existing → に)

Same noun, three different particles, all because the verb changed.

## More side-by-side pairs

> としょかん **に** いきます。
> *I go to the library.* (motion → に)

> としょかん **で** べんきょう します。
> *I study at the library.* (action → で)

---

> いえ **に** います。
> *I'm at home.* (existence → に)

> いえ **で** ねます。
> *I sleep at home.* (action → で)

---

> がっこう **に** きました。
> *I came to school.* (motion → に)

> がっこう **で** にほんご を べんきょう します。
> *I study Japanese at school.* (action → で)

## The decision tree

When you want "at / in [place]":

1. Is the verb いきます / きます / かえります / つきます (arrive)?
   → **に**
2. Is the verb います / あります? → **に**
3. Is the verb something you **do** there (eat, study, play, work,
   sleep, read, watch)? → **で**
