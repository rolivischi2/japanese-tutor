# か — the sentence-final question marker

The particle **か** sits at the very end of a sentence and turns it into
a **question**. No word-order changes are needed — you just glue か onto
the end and (in writing) usually skip the question mark.

## Pattern

```
[statement] + か。
```

That's it. The rest of the sentence stays in the same order it would
have if it were a statement.

## Examples

> がくせい です → がくせい ですか。
> *Is (someone) a student?*

> あなた は にほんじん です → あなた は にほんじん ですか。
> *Are you Japanese?*

> これ は ほん です → これ は ほん ですか。
> *Is this a book?*

> いきます → いきますか。
> *Will you go?*

> ともだち は きました → ともだち は きましたか。
> *Did your friend come?*

## With question words

Even when the sentence contains a question word (なに, だれ, どこ,
いつ, いくら, etc.), you still need か at the end.

> なん ですか。
> *What is it?*

> どこ に いきますか。
> *Where are you going?*

> いつ きましたか。
> *When did (you/they) come?*

> いくら ですか。
> *How much is it?*

## Punctuation

Japanese traditionally writes the sentence-ending か with a regular
period 「。」 and **no question mark**. The question mark (?) does
appear in casual writing, manga, and chat, but textbook style is just
「ですか。」.

## Casual speech — drop か, raise pitch

In **casual** conversation, you very often drop the か and signal the
question with **rising intonation** alone. The trade-off: it sounds
casual / familiar, not polite.

> Polite: がくせい ですか。 (rising or flat)
> Casual: がくせい？ ↗ (rising)

For Module 01 / polite-form work, **keep か**. Save the casual drop for
when you're talking with peers and the register is clearly informal.

## A subtle nicety

You can soften an otherwise-blunt answer by tucking a soft か onto a
self-directed thought ("hmm, is that so…"), but that's a later-module
nuance. For now, treat か as **"question mark in particle form."**
