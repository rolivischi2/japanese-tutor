# が — the subject particle

The particle **が** marks the **grammatical subject** — the doer or the
thing that exists. Where は says "let's talk about X," が says
"**X** is the one."

が tends to introduce **new** information ("there's a cat over there!"),
while は assumes the topic is already on the table.

## Pattern

```
[subject] が [predicate]
```

Often the が phrase is the new piece of news in the sentence — the
listener didn't know it yet.

## Examples — new information

> ねこ が います。
> *There's a cat.* (announcing the cat — new info)

> あめ が ふっています。
> *It's raining.* (the rain is the news)

> でんわ が なりました。
> *The phone rang.* (the phone is new in the moment)

> やまださん が きました。
> *Yamada-san came.* (the question was "who came?" — answer: Yamada-san)

## Examples — existence verbs (あります / います)

When you announce that something **exists** somewhere, the existing thing
takes が. This is non-negotiable.

> つくえ の うえ に ほん が あります。
> *There's a book on the desk.*

> こうえん に こども が います。
> *There are children in the park.*

> れいぞうこ に たまご が あります。
> *There are eggs in the fridge.*

## Examples — asking and answering "who/what?"

When the question word (だれ "who", なに "what") is the subject, both
the question and the answer use が — never は.

> だれ が きましたか。— やまださん が きました。
> *Who came? — Yamada-san came.*

> なに が ありますか。— おちゃ が あります。
> *What's there? — There's tea.*
