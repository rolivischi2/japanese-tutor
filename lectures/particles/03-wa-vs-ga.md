# は vs が — the canonical confusion

This is the single trickiest pair of particles in beginner Japanese. The
short version:

- **は** marks the **topic** — "as for X, …". Old / known information.
- **が** marks the **subject** — "X (specifically) is the one". New /
  contrastive information, and the answer to "who/what?".

## Quick test

Try replacing the particle slot with English **"as for"** vs **"it's X
that"**:

| If you mean… | Use… |
| --- | --- |
| "**As for** sushi, I like it." | は |
| "**It's** sushi (specifically) **that** I like." | が |

## Side-by-side examples

> わたし は がくせい です。
> *I am a student.* (introducing yourself — you are the topic)

> わたし が がくせい です。
> *I am the student.* (someone asked who the student is — answer: me)

---

> やまださん は きました。
> *Yamada-san came.* (we were already talking about Yamada-san)

> やまださん が きました。
> *Yamada-san came!* / *It was Yamada-san who came.* (news, or answer to "who came?")

---

> ねこ は います。
> *Cats? Yeah, there are some.* (the topic "cats" was already in play)

> ねこ が います。
> *There's a cat!* (announcing — new information)

---

> きょう は あつい です。
> *Today is hot.* (today is the topic; comment is "hot")

> きょう が あつい です。
> *Today is the hot one.* (compared to other days — contrastive)

## Weather and natural phenomena

Weather sentences default to **が** because they typically announce news:

> あめ が ふっています。
> *It's raining.*

> ゆき が ふりました。
> *It snowed.*

> かぜ が つよい です。
> *The wind is strong.*

## Question words

If the question word is the **subject**, both question and answer use が.
If the question word is the **predicate** (asking "what is X?"), the
known thing keeps は.

> だれ が きましたか。— やまださん が きました。
> *Who came? — Yamada-san came.* (subject is unknown → が)

> これ は なん ですか。— ほん です。
> *What is this? — A book.* (we know what "this" refers to → は;
> the predicate "what" is the unknown)

## Rule of thumb

When in doubt, use **は**. It's the unmarked, neutral choice for stating
something about a known topic. Use **が** when you're announcing news,
answering "who/what?", or it's an existence verb (あります / います).
