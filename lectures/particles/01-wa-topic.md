# は — the topic particle

The particle **は** marks the **topic** of the sentence — the thing the
rest of the sentence is going to say something about. The cleanest mental
gloss is **"as for ___,"**.

> Pronunciation: when the kana は is used as the topic particle, it is
> read **"wa"** — not "ha". Everywhere else (inside ordinary words like
> はな "flower" or はは "mother"), it stays "ha". This pronunciation
> quirk is a fossil of older Japanese spelling.

## Pattern

```
[topic] は [predicate] 。
```

The predicate ends in です, an adjective, or a verb. The topic comes
first because Japanese front-loads what the sentence is about.

## Examples

> わたし は がくせい です。
> *I am a student.* (As for me, student.)

> あなた は にほんじん ですか。
> *Are you Japanese?* (As for you, Japanese?)

> これ は ほん です。
> *This is a book.* (As for this thing, book.)

> きょう は あつい です。
> *It's hot today.* (As for today, hot.)

> やまださん は せんせい です。
> *Yamada-san is a teacher.* (As for Yamada-san, teacher.)

Notice that the topic is often **not** the grammatical subject in the
English translation — "today" is the topic in the fourth example, not
the subject of "hot." Japanese lets you put almost anything up front as
the topic and then make a comment about it.

## What it does NOT do

- It does **not** add stress or emphasis like English "as for".
- It does **not** announce a new subject — for that you want が
  (next lecture).
- It does **not** appear inside questions like "**who** is the teacher?"
  — there, the unknown takes が, not は.
