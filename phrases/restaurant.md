# Restaurant

Ordering food, complimenting the meal, asking for the check. Enough to
get through a casual dinner without panicking.

## Sitting down and ordering

> メニュー を ください。
> *Menu, please.*

> これ ください。
> *This one, please.* (point at the menu; the simplest possible order)

> おすすめ は なん ですか。
> *What do you recommend?* (literally "what is the recommendation?")

> ビール を ください。
> *A beer, please.*

> みず を ください。
> *Water, please.*

## During the meal

> いただきます。
> *I gratefully receive.* (said before eating — a fixed pre-meal phrase
> with no real English equivalent. Do not skip it.)

> おいしい です。
> *It's delicious.*

> おなか が すきました。
> *I'm hungry.* (literally "my stomach has emptied")

> おなか が いっぱい です。
> *I'm full.*

## Finishing up

> ごちそうさま でした。
> *Thank you for the meal.* (said when you're done eating; the partner
> to いただきます — also fixed, never skip it)

> おかんじょう おねがいします。
> *The check, please.*

> べつべつ で おねがいします。
> *Separate checks, please.* (handy for group dinners; many casual
> places don't actually do this, but it's understood)

## Notes on register

- **いただきます** and **ごちそうさま でした** are non-optional in a
  Japanese cultural sense — even alone, even with a convenience-store
  bento, the muscle memory is to say them.
- **ください** literally means "please give me" — works with any noun
  + を.
- The check is usually paid at the **register near the door**, not at
  the table. The phrase おかんじょう おねがいします signals you're
  done; the staff will bring the slip to you or wave you up front.
