# Agreeing and refusing

The minimal toolkit for saying yes / no / "got it" / "I don't know" /
"it's fine." Useful in literally every conversation.

## Yes / no

> はい。
> *Yes.* (polite, all-purpose)

> いいえ。
> *No.* (polite — but Japanese speakers often soften "no" with a phrase
> like ちょっと… instead of a flat いいえ; see notes below)

> ええ。
> *Yeah. / Yes.* (slightly softer / warmer than はい; common in casual
> and semi-formal speech)

## Confirming / denying a statement

> そう です。
> *That's right.* / *Yes, that's so.*

> そう ですね。
> *That's right, isn't it. / I agree.* (with thinking pause)

> ちがいます。
> *That's wrong. / No, that's not it.* (use when correcting a factual
> mistake — softer than a flat いいえ)

## Understanding

> わかりました。
> *I understand. / Got it.* (use when someone has just explained something)

> わかりません。
> *I don't understand.* (essential — don't be afraid to say it)

> わかります か。
> *Do you understand?*

## "It's fine / no problem"

> だいじょうぶ です。
> *It's fine. / I'm OK. / No problem.* (one of the most useful set
> phrases — fits "no thanks", "I'm fine", "don't worry about it", and
> "it's safe")

## Notes on register

- A flat **いいえ** can sound abrupt. In real conversation you often
  soften with **ちょっと…** ("um, a bit…"), **すみません** ("sorry"),
  or **ちがいます** when correcting.
- **そう です** vs **そう です ね** — the ね adds a soft, agreeing
  "isn't it" tone, the closest English has to "right!". Without ね it's
  a plain "yes, that's correct."
- **だいじょうぶ です** is the Swiss-Army knife of polite refusals: when
  a clerk asks if you want a bag, "だいじょうぶ です" politely means
  "no thanks, I'm good."
