# Directions

Asking for a place, understanding the directions back. Stations and
landmarks come up constantly in Japan.

## Asking where something is

> すみません、えき は どこ ですか。
> *Excuse me, where is the station?* (すみません = "excuse me" — the
> universal opener for asking strangers anything)

> すみません、トイレ は どこ ですか。
> *Excuse me, where's the bathroom?*

> えき まで どう いきますか。
> *How do I get to the station?* (まで = "as far as / up to")

## Distance

> ちかい です。
> *It's close.*

> とおい です。
> *It's far.*

> あるいて いけます か。
> *Can I walk there?* (lit. "can I go on foot?")

## Direction words

> みぎ。
> *Right.*

> ひだり。
> *Left.*

> まっすぐ。
> *Straight ahead.*

> まえ。
> *In front.*

> うしろ。
> *Behind.*

## Position relative to landmarks

> コンビニ の となり です。
> *It's next to the convenience store.*

> ぎんこう の まえ です。
> *It's in front of the bank.*

## Useful follow-ups

> もう いちど おねがいします。
> *One more time, please.* (when the answer came too fast — extremely
> useful when asking directions in any language you don't fully know)

> ゆっくり おねがいします。
> *Slowly, please.*

## Notes

- People in Japan are usually willing to help with directions, sometimes
  to the extent of walking you there. A small bow + ありがとう ございます
  at the end goes a long way.
- For longer directions, accept that you'll catch maybe half — the key
  is to repeat the landmark (コンビニ? ぎんこう?) so the person can
  confirm.
