# Mnemonic Template

Guidance for writing custom kanji mnemonics. Follow these principles for maximum retention.

## Principles

### Visual and Concrete
TODO: The mnemonic should paint a vivid picture in your mind. Abstract descriptions do not stick. "A roof (𠆢) over a good (良) meal — eating" works for 食 because it is spatial and concrete.

### Short
TODO: Maximum 1–2 sentences. Long mnemonics are hard to recall under pressure.

### Weird or Emotionally Charged
TODO: Bizarre, funny, or slightly shocking mnemonics are remembered longer than mundane ones. Do not self-censor for weirdness.

### Reuse Stock Characters
TODO: Build a personal cast of recurring characters for common radicals. Example suggestions:
- 人 = "the developer" (a software engineer — or substitute any recurring character from your own life)
- 口 = "the cafe" (a place associated with that character)
- 日 = "a laptop screen" (backlit rectangle)
- 木 = "a git tree" (branching structure)
These are starting suggestions — substitute your own cast of vivid characters.

### Tie to Existing Vocab
TODO: Every mnemonic must link the kanji to a Japanese word already known from audio/kana context, not to an abstract English keyword. Example: for 食, the mnemonic connects to たべる (to eat) and しょくじ (meal) — both words known from audio before the kanji is introduced.

## Template

```
Kanji: [character]
Primary meaning: [English]
Radicals: [list]
Mnemonic: [1–2 sentences, visual, weird, linking to known vocab]
Vocab anchors: [たべる, しょくじ, ...]
```

## Example

```
Kanji: 食
Primary meaning: eat / food
Radicals: 𠆢 (roof/crown), 良 (good)
Mnemonic: A roof (𠆢) over a good (良) meal — the developer eats (たべる) under the cafe awning.
Vocab anchors: たべる, しょくじ, しょくどう
```
