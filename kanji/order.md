# Kanji Introduction Order

## Ordering Principle: Vocab Frequency, Not JLPT or Radical Complexity

TODO: Explain the guiding principle: kanji are introduced in the order that their associated vocabulary appears in the learner's study, which follows conversational frequency rather than JLPT level or radical/stroke complexity. A kanji is introduced when its most common associated vocab word enters the learner's active or passive vocabulary.

## Why Not WaniKani / RTK Order

TODO: Explain the decision not to follow WaniKani's radical-complexity ordering or Heisig's RTK keyword ordering:
- WaniKani teaches kanji readings in isolation before the associated vocabulary — context-free learning that slows retention.
- RTK trains production (keyword → handwriting) which is irrelevant to this curriculum's spoken-fluency goal.
- Frequency-driven + vocab-grounded order ensures every kanji learned is immediately useful.
