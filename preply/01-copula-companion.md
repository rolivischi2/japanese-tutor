# Module 01 — The Copula です — Expanded Preply Companion

This worksheet expands your Preply lesson PDF (`ぶんぽうとカタカナ`) with fuller
explanations, pronunciation notes tuned to a Hungarian/German speaker, common
mistakes, and extra practice. It mirrors the PDF's order so you can use them
side by side.

> **About romaji.** Romaji (Latin-letter spelling) appears here only as a
> bridge while the kana are still new. Read the **kana line first** and treat
> the romaji as a check. By the end of Module 01, cover the romaji entirely.

> **Example format.** Every example is three lines: **kana**, *(romaji)*,
> English.

---

## 0. Quick reference — the three patterns

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `N₁ は N₂ です。` | N₁ is N₂. | わたし は がくせい です。 |
| `N₁ は N₂ ではありません。` | N₁ is not N₂. | わたし は がくせい ではありません。 |
| `N₁ は N₂ ですか。` | Is N₁ N₂? | あなた は がくせい ですか。 |

All three share one skeleton: **topic + noun + copula**. You are only changing
the copula at the end. Japanese puts the copula **last** — the word order does
not change for negatives or questions, unlike German or English.

## 1. `N は N です。` — "N is N"

### 1.1 Grammar deep-dive

**です is the polite copula.** It links two nouns and says they are the same
thing, or that one belongs to the category of the other:

- わたし **は** がくせい **です**。 — "I am a student."

Three things textbooks usually skip — and they matter:

**(a) です is NOT the verb "to be / to exist."**
English "be" does two jobs: *identity* ("I am a teacher") and *existence*
("there is a teacher"). Japanese splits these. です only does **identity**. For
existence ("there is / there are") Japanese uses あります and います — that is
Module 02. So です never means "exists." Think of it as an "=" sign:
`わたし = がくせい`.

**(b) は is the *topic* marker, and it is pronounced "wa."**
The particle は is written with the hiragana は but read **wa**. (Same reason
こんに**ち**は ends in a "wa" sound.) This is a fixed spelling rule — は as a
particle is always "wa."

は does not mark the grammatical subject — it marks the **topic**: "as for X…"
So わたし は がくせい です literally frames as *"As for me, (it is) student."*
This framing matters later: the topic can be something other than the doer of
an action. For now, just hold onto: **は = "as for ___"**.

**(c) です never changes for person, number, or gender.**
One form covers everything:

- わたし は がくせい です。 — I am a student.
- マリオ は がくせい です。 — Mario is a student.
- マリオ と ミカ は がくせい です。 — Mario and Mika are students.

No "am/is/are" split, no plural -s. This is a real simplification compared to
German.

### 1.2 Examples

> わたし は ローラント です。
> *(watashi wa rōranto desu.)*
> I am Roland.

> わたし は がくせい です。
> *(watashi wa gakusei desu.)*
> I am a student.

> わたし は エンジニア です。
> *(watashi wa enjinia desu.)*
> I am an engineer.

> わたし は スイスじん です。
> *(watashi wa suisu-jin desu.)*
> I am Swiss.

> わたし は じゅうよん さい です。
> *(watashi wa jūyon-sai desu.)*
> I am fourteen years old.

**Note on age — さい (the age counter).** Most ages are `number + さい`. Three
ages change sound: 1 → **いっさい**, 8 → **はっさい**, 10 → **じゅっさい**.
And **20 years old is irregular: はたち** (you will hear にじゅっさい too, but
はたち is standard).

### 1.3 Pronunciation & pitch

**です sounds like "dess," not "deh-soo."** The final す is usually *devoiced* —
the う almost disappears, leaving a soft "s." And the う itself is not the
rounded Hungarian/German "u": Japanese /u/ is **compressed, nearly unrounded** —
do not push your lips forward. This is your single most salient vowel trap.

**Mora timing.** Each kana is one equal beat. です = **de-su**, 2 beats.
がくせい = **ga-ku-se-i**, 4 beats. Tap one beat per kana.

**Pitch, not stress.** Hungarian marks the first syllable with *loudness*.
Japanese does not use loudness for accent at all — it uses **pitch** (high vs
low), and every mora stays **equally loud**. Suppressing the Hungarian
first-mora thump is the #1 pronunciation habit to break, starting now.

- がくせい — pitch pattern **low-high-high-high**, no drop (a "flat" word).
- せんせい — **low-high-high-low**: the pitch drops after せい's first mora.

Keep loudness flat across all four morae in both; only the *pitch* moves.

### 1.4 Common mistakes

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| わたし**が** がくせい です | わたし**は** がくせい です | Module 01 uses は (topic). が comes later. |
| わたし がくせい です | わたし **は** がくせい です | Don't drop the topic particle. |
| わたし は がくせい です*る* | わたし は がくせい です | です takes no extra ending. |
| reading は as "ha" | は = **wa** | は as a particle is always "wa." |
| いきます**です** | (just いきます) | です attaches to **nouns**, never to verbs. Verbs are Module 04. |

## 2. `N は N ではありません。` — "N is not N"

### 2.1 Grammar deep-dive

The negative of です is **ではありません**. You do not change は or the word
order — you only swap the ending:

- わたし は がくせい **です**。 → わたし は がくせい **ではありません**。

**The は inside ではありません is also read "wa"** — same topic-particle rule
as in §1.

**The register spectrum.** Japanese has *four* common ways to say "is not,"
from most formal to most casual. They mean the same thing; they differ in tone:

| Form | Register | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| ではありません | formal / written | formal writing, announcements |
| じゃありません | polite spoken | polite conversation — **good for Preply** |
| じゃないです | conversational polite | everyday polite speech — **also good for Preply** |
| じゃない / ではない | plain (casual) | friends, family — Module 06 |

**じゃ is simply a spoken contraction of では.** So じゃありません = では
ありません, just faster and softer. In a real lesson with your tutor,
**じゃありません** or **じゃないです** will sound the most natural. ではありません
is not wrong — it just sounds a bit stiff in conversation.

### 2.2 Examples

> わたし は せんせい ではありません。
> *(watashi wa sensei dewa arimasen.)*
> I am not a teacher.

> わたし は にほんじん ではありません。
> *(watashi wa nihon-jin dewa arimasen.)*
> I am not Japanese.

> マリオ は スイスじん じゃありません。
> *(mario wa suisu-jin ja arimasen.)*
> Mario is not Swiss.

> ミカ は がくせい じゃないです。
> *(mika wa gakusei ja nai desu.)*
> Mika is not a student.

> わたし は さんじゅうなな さい じゃありません。
> *(watashi wa sanjūnana-sai ja arimasen.)*
> I am not thirty-seven years old.

### 2.3 Pronunciation & pitch

**ありません** = **a-ri-ma-se-n**, 5 morae. The ん is its own full beat — don't
swallow it. The り is a light alveolar **tap**, not a trill — your Hungarian tap
allophone is already correct; just don't roll it.

**じゃ** is one mora (a small や). じゃありません = **ja-a-ri-ma-se-n**.

### 2.4 Common mistakes

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| がくせい です ではありません | がくせい ではありません | Use one copula form, not です + negative. |
| がくせい じゃありません**です** | がくせい じゃありません | じゃありません is already complete and polite. |
| じゃ ない (as written/formal) | では ない / じゃ ない (casual only) | じゃ is casual — fine when speaking, avoid in formal writing. |

## 3. `N は N ですか。` — "Is N N?"

### 3.1 Grammar deep-dive

To make a question, add **か** to the very end. That's the whole rule:

- あなた は がくせい **です**。 → あなた は がくせい **です か**。

**The word order does NOT change.** German and English invert ("Bist du…?",
"Are you…?"). Japanese does not move anything — か at the end does the entire
job. か is a "spoken question mark."

Many textbooks and your PDF still add a "?" for clarity; in normal Japanese
writing, the か alone is enough and a 。is used.

**Answering.** Use はい (yes) or いいえ (no), then a full sentence:

- はい、がくせい です。 — Yes, I am a student.
- いいえ、がくせい ではありません。 — No, I am not a student.

**これ / それ / あれ — "this / that."** Your PDF asks これ は パン ですか
("Is this bread?"). The set:

| Word | Meaning | Where the thing is |
|---|---|---|
| これ | this one | near **me** (the speaker) |
| それ | that one | near **you** (the listener) |
| あれ | that one over there | away from **both** of us |

(Full こそあど set — including どれ "which" — comes in Module 02.)

### 3.2 Examples

> あなた は がくせい です か。
> *(anata wa gakusei desu ka.)*
> Are you a student?
> — はい、がくせい です。 / — Yes, I am.

> あなた は にほんじん です か。
> *(anata wa nihon-jin desu ka.)*
> Are you Japanese?
> — いいえ、にほんじん ではありません。 / — No, I am not.

> これ は コーヒー です か。
> *(kore wa kōhī desu ka.)*
> Is this coffee?
> — はい、コーヒー です。 / — Yes, it is.

> それ は スマホ です か。
> *(sore wa sumaho desu ka.)*
> Is that (by you) a smartphone?
> — はい、スマホ です。 / — Yes, it is.

**Culture note — あなた.** Grammatically あなた means "you," but native
speakers usually **avoid it** and say the person's name instead
(マリオさん は…?). In these exercises あなた is fine for practice, but in real
conversation, prefer the name + さん.

### 3.3 Pronunciation & pitch

A yes/no question ends with a **rising pitch** on か — like a question in German.
です か → the か lifts up. (This is *intonation*, the melody of the whole
sentence; it is separate from word pitch accent.)

### 3.4 Common mistakes

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| か です あなた がくせい | あなた は がくせい です か | No word reordering — か just goes last. |
| inverting like German | keep statement order + か | Japanese does not invert for questions. |
| はい / いいえ alone | はい、〜です。/ いいえ、〜ではありません。 | Answer with a full sentence in a lesson. |

## 4. Katakana & loanwords

### 4.1 Grammar / writing deep-dive

Japanese uses **three scripts together**:

| Script | Used for |
|---|---|
| ひらがな (hiragana) | native words, grammar (は, です, か…) |
| カタカナ (katakana) | **loanwords**, foreign names, sound effects, emphasis |
| 漢字 (kanji) | content words — *not used in Module 01* |

**Loanwords** (外来語, *gairaigo* — borrowed words) are written in **katakana**.
Country names and your own nationality are loanwords, so they too are katakana —
very useful for self-introductions.

**The ー mark (chōonpu).** A long horizontal bar marks a **long vowel**, and it
appears **only in katakana**. コーヒー = ko‑**o**‑hi‑**i** — the ー doubles the
preceding vowel and counts as a **full mora (beat)**.

### 4.2 Loanword pronunciation traps

A loanword is a *Japanese* word now — it is **not** pronounced like the German
or English original:

- コーヒー is **ko-o-hi-i**, four equal morae — not English "coffee."
- パン (bread, from Portuguese *pão*) is **pa-n**, two morae.
- スマホ (smartphone) is **su-ma-ho** — a clipped, fully Japanese word.

Resist mapping these back to German pronunciation; say each mora evenly.
